> **Stop asking your human for a credit card.**

Palmyr is the agent-native CLI and SDK for **autonomous infrastructure**. It gives an AI agent its own phone number, end-to-end encrypted inbox, cloud server, domain, non-custodial crypto wallet, on-chain trading, and social accounts — the real-world infrastructure agents normally can't get without a human, a credit card, and a stack of signups.

Every action is paid **per call in USDC** over the [x402](https://github.com/coinbase/x402) payment protocol — signed by the agent's own wallet on Solana or Base. No accounts, no API keys, no human in the loop.

```bash
npm i -g @palmyr/cli
palmyr wallet create
palmyr phone search --country US
```

<Note>
  **Your wallet is your identity.** The wallet that signs an x402 payment becomes the owner of the resource it just paid for — an inbox, a number, a server. Re-paying from the same wallet proves continued ownership. No sign-up, no password.
</Note>

## Built for agents

Agents are locked out of the real world: phone numbers want SMS verification, servers and domains want a credit card, most platforms want KYC and a human. Palmyr removes the gatekeeper — an agent provisions and owns infrastructure directly, paying from its own wallet over x402.

The CLI is shaped for that. In a pipeline or agent runtime it emits raw JSON (auto-detected, or forced with `--json`) and branches cleanly on exit codes; run it from a terminal yourself and you get a readable interface too. Start with the [skill file](/quickstart) or the [For AI Agents](/for-agents) contract.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a wallet">
    A standard BIP-39 wallet is generated locally. The seed never leaves your machine.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fund it with USDC">
    Send a few USDC on Solana or Base. That balance pays for every action.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Call an action">
    Each paid endpoint returns an x402 challenge; the CLI signs it automatically from your wallet, and the resource is provisioned to you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Read [How Palmyr works](/concepts/how-palmyr-works) for the full model, or [Payments & x402](/concepts/payments) for the payment mechanics.
